# Verification of Section 9 (bada)

An independent verifier reviewed the finished scene from rendered frames. Their findings follow. The director has read them and added rulings; where a ruling differs from the art brief, the ruling wins.

## Verdict

Not ready to show. The timing, crossings, coverage, title size and purity and speed all pass, and the bar-2 pelican eye roll is correct in timing. But the fish's answering eye roll is too small to see, and much of the brushwork reads as clean vector line rather than wet and dry brush: the eye ring, bill, bicycle and inscription. The single most important fix is to make the fish's eye roll unmistakable at 1080p. Right after that, separate the pelican from the rock in bar 1.

## The director's rulings

1. The fish must answer: eye ring about 9 units across, pupil about 3 units, rolling all the way into the upper back corner between u = 3.2 and 3.8 with white showing beneath it. It must be visible in an ordinary full-size frame.
2. The rock moves out of the pelican's path. Nothing of it may lie between y = 560 and y = 705. Preferred: an overhanging, top-heavy rock coming down from the top edge at the upper left (about x 20 - 240, y 433 - 552), so the pelican enters beneath it; keep at least 40 units clear of the inscription. Remove the reserve cut that was needed while they overlapped. Give the rock a broken, angular contour and dry texture strokes, not a mushroom cap.
3. The pelican's eye: one dry stroke for the ring, heavy on the lid, thinning and stopping short, with a visible gap; radius about 10 units so the roll reads at film speed. The bill: two strokes that press at the base and break into flying white towards the tip.
4. The bicycle: three or four dry strokes of varied pressure for the frame; each wheel one ring that plainly stops short of closing, with no hairline completing the circle; two or three tapered spokes are enough.
5. The inscription goes through the ink sheet with a light bleed and tone that varies from character to character; a brushed, slightly open ring instead of a typeset zero; the oval seal at the foot of the signature column; the English lines below the columns, across.
6. The lotus leaf: one continuous torn form, the dark wet half flowing into the pale half, veins starting from the stem.
7. Minor: reduce the even grey halo round the neck and body (less bleed reach on the pelican's sheet).

## Findings

### [must-fix] The fish's answering eye-roll is unreadable

Evidence: verify-bada-fisheyes.png: the fish pupil is about 6 px across inside a square eye about 25 px wide and moves only about 4-5 px from u=3.0 to u=3.8. In the full frames verify-bada-u3.0.png and verify-bada-56.8.png the fish's eye change cannot be seen at all. The brief requires that the fish rolls its own eye up to match, and the checklist requires that the fish answers.

Suggested fix: Make the fish eye about 2x larger (ring about 8-9 u, pupil about 3 u) and push the pupil all the way into the upper back corner with clear white beneath, like the pelican. Consider lowering a lid stroke at the same time. Check it in a full-size frame at u=3.6.

### [should-fix] Pelican tangled with the rock for most of bar 1

Evidence: frame_003 (u=0.5): the bill tip runs into the rock's top contour and the front wheel crosses the rock's black left contour. frame_005 (u=1.0) and run sheet 53.5-54.0: the body wash and wing merge into the rock's grey washes. At u=1.5 (verify-bada-eyes.png) a hard straight-edged white cut and a black wedge show where the knockout clips the rock. The bird is hard to read and the knockout has a clean geometric edge.

Suggested fix: Move or shrink the rock so its head sits behind the pelican's path only briefly: lower its top below about y 600, or shift it to x ≤ 160. Alternatively, make the reserve a soft, irregular, wet-edged knockout with no straight cut, and lighten the rock wash wherever the bird crosses it.

### [should-fix] Eye ring and bill read as spectacles and ruled lines, not brush

Evidence: verify-bada-eyes.png: the eye is a fully closed rounded rectangle with an even thin line on three sides and a heavy flat bar on top. Next to the bill it reads as glasses, and the brief asks for a ring that is not quite closed. The bill is two even parallel black bars with little flying white or pressure change. The pouch has a thin dark outline along its bottom, which breaks the brief's rule of no outline drawn round a form.

Suggested fix: Paint the eye as one dry stroke that starts heavy on the lid, thins and stops short, leaving a visible gap at the lower back. Let the bill strokes press at the base and break into flying white toward the tip, and let the lower bill stroke fade before the tip. Drop the pouch outline and leave the pouch as a single pale wet touch.

### [should-fix] Bicycle drawn with mechanical, even line

Evidence: verify-bada-u3.0.png and verify-bada-56.8.png: the frame is thin straight lines with fine hatching, like a technical drawing. The wheels are complete hairline circles with a heavy arc over part of them, so the ring closes, against the brief's 'ring that does not quite close'. The spokes are ruler-straight. This is the most visibly digital element in the frame.

Suggested fix: Paint the frame as three or four dry strokes of varied pressure that overshoot or stop short at the joints. Let each wheel ring stop clearly short of closing, with no hairline completing it. Give the spokes a slight taper and break, or reduce them to two or three.

### [should-fix] Inscription looks typeset rather than brushed

Evidence: verify-bada-titlecrop.png: Xingkai SC in uniform jet black with crisp vector edges and no ink density change or bleed into the xuan. The 〇 is a perfect geometric circle. The oval seal floats mid-column between 写意 and the date instead of sitting under the signature. The English sits beside the date column, not 'below, across' as the brief places it.

Suggested fix: Lay the inscription through the ink sheet with a light bleedSheet pass and per-glyph tone variation (some characters nong, some running dry). Replace 〇 with a slightly open brushed ring. Move the oval seal to the foot of the 八大山人 column and set the English lines below the Chinese columns.

### [should-fix] Rock and lotus leaf read as odd objects

Evidence: frame_002 and frame_005: the rock is a tall mushroom or molar shape. A strong black 'C' bracket stroke on its right side and eyebrow-like interior strokes make it look cartoonish rather than a top-heavy Bada rock. verify-bada-58.75.png: the leaf is a dark blob plus a separate pale fan, with veins radiating from a hard black node, so it reads as a bow-tie or moth. The bud on the second stem reads as a drooping pod.

Suggested fix: Give the rock a broken, angular contour with dry scumbled texture strokes and one undercut, and remove the interior arcs. Make the leaf one continuous torn form with the dark half flowing into the pale half, and the veins starting at the stem attachment rather than at a black dot. Make the bud a clear pointed ovoid in two strokes.

### [minor] Soft halo around the neck and body looks like a drop shadow

Evidence: verify-bada-56.8.png and verify-bada-eyes.png: an even grey blur band runs along both sides of the neck stroke and round the head. It reads as a uniform glow or double contour, not ink creeping along paper fibres.

Suggested fix: Reduce the bleed reach on the pelican sheet, or make the bleed noisy and directional (fibre-like). Keep the knockout or reserve the same tone as the paper so no band shows.

### [minor] Pelican eye roll is small on screen

Evidence: verify-bada-u3.0.png: the eye ring is about 45x50 px at 1080p and the pupil travels about 20 px. It reads only if you are looking for it, so the joke lands weakly at film speed.

Suggested fix: Increase the eye to about R 10 u and the pupil travel, or make the lid stroke lower further (about 45%), so the white beneath is unmistakable.

## Items of the brief's checklist that failed

- **Every mark reads as one stroke of a wet or a dry brush; ink has bled into the paper at wet edges**: Wet bleed is real at the rock washes, the leaf pool, the pelican body and the fish back (soft halos in verify-bada-u3.0.png and verify-bada-56.8.png). But many marks read as vector lines, not brush strokes. The eye ring is a neat closed rounded rectangle; the bill is two even parallel bars; the bicycle frame is thin ruled lines with hatching; the spokes are ruler-straight; the pouch and the fish belly are thin outlines. All of these are visible in verify-bada-eyes.png and verify-bada-fisheyes.png.
- **With so few strokes it is still at once a pelican on a bicycle**: In bar 2 (verify-bada-u3.0.png) it reads as a long-billed bird on a bicycle, but more heron than pelican: the pouch is a faint pale wash with a thin underline. The square eye ring under its heavy lid bar reads as spectacles. In bar 1 (frame_003 at u=0.5, frame_005 at u=1.0) the bill and body merge into the rock wash, so it is not readable at a glance.
- **The fish answers**: verify-bada-fisheyes.png (crops of the fish at 2x). The pupil is about 6 px across inside a square about 25 px wide and moves roughly 4-5 px between u=3.0 and u=3.8. At 1080p film speed the answer is effectively invisible. At u=5.2 the pupil still looks high, so the return is not clearly readable either.
