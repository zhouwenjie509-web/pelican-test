# Verification of Section 13 (scream)

An independent verifier reviewed the finished scene from rendered frames. Their findings follow. The director has read them and added rulings; where a ruling differs from the art brief or from a suggested fix, the ruling wins.

## Verdict

Close to ready. Timing is right: no bell strokes at u=2.90, bell strokes at 2.95, he turns between 3.00 and 3.10, and the wave leaves his head from 3.05. Both crossings line up with no pop, the title is legible and in view more than 2 s, and purity and speed pass. It should not be shown until the two distant figures are moved off the pelican's line: at u≈0.5–0.7 the dark bicycle rides straight through the top-hat figure's dark coat and merges with it (§4.2). After that, the ugly T-pose in-between at u=3.05 and the cartoon-ring wave are the main art weaknesses.

## The director's rulings

1. The two distant figures come off the pelican's line: feet at about y 668, about 68 units tall, set beyond the railing and a step cooler and paler (blue-grey), built from vertical crayon strokes with no face discs. The dark bicycle must read against them at u = 0.5 - 0.8.
2. The passer-by stands lower: the top of his head at about y 660, so that as the pelican passes behind him its body, neck and head stay in view above his head and only the wheels are covered. He is a bust cut by the bottom edge. Before the beat: three-quarter back view, shoulders relaxed, arms down out of the picture, a cheek and an ear showing on the turned side. Keep him at about x = 640, clear of the pelican on the beat itself.
3. The turn has no ugly in-between. Either cut through one held jerk pose (shoulders up, head half round) for two or three frames, or carry the hands up along the body to the cheeks and let the hair go with the head. It is complete within a tenth of a second of u = 3.0.
4. The wave bends the landscape itself: as the front passes, the band strokes of the sky and the fjord are displaced and heightened in colour. No overlaid rings of dashes, no radial or vertical dashes left behind.
5. Sky and fjord: strokes three to five times longer along their bands, less colour noise inside a band, so five or six broad wavering bands of sky and one strong S-curving pale shoreline read at once. It must not look like the Starry Night next door.
6. Minor: give the rails a slight waver; redraw the pelican's tail as two or three short tapering strokes.

## Findings

### [must-fix] The two distant figures stand on the pelican's line and merge with the bicycle

Evidence: 77.5 (sheet frame_003) and 77.6 (crop out/shots/verify-scream-77.6-crop.png): the top-hat figure's feet are on the same baseline as the wheels (screen y about 820). The front wheel and the dark-blue frame pass straight through his near-black coat, dark line over dark coat, so wheel and figure fuse into one shape for about 0.4 s. This breaks guide §4.2 (nothing in y 560 to 705 the pelican could merge with) and the brief's 'beyond the pelican's path'.

Suggested fix: Set the far figures well back: feet at road y about 670 to 680, scaled down about 15%. Or move them behind the near railing posts at x about 60 to 90 so the pelican reaches them only after the crossing. Lighten or blue-shift their coats so the dark bicycle line keeps contrast wherever they overlap.

### [should-fix] Grotesque in-between frame during the turn

Evidence: 80.05 (u=3.05), crop out/shots/verify-scream-80.05-crop.png: the mix of the two point sets leaves a dark side-swept hair patch over the forehead of a half-skull face, with both hands sticking straight out sideways at hip height like a T-pose. It is visible for about 3 frames at 60 fps and reads as a glitch, not a start.

Suggested fix: Do not interpolate hair or hands linearly. Make the hair vanish or swing round with the head in the first 0.03 s. Route the hands up along the body (hem, then chest, then cheeks) instead of straight out. Or cut on 2s: one held 'jerk' pose (shoulders up, head mid-turn) for 0.05 s, then the final pose.

### [should-fix] Pelican almost entirely hidden behind the passer-by

Evidence: 80.8 (u=3.8): only the head and neck show above his skull. The whole body, both wheels and the legs are behind his head, hands and coat. Hidden for about u 3.6 to 4.0, and at that moment the frame does not read as a pelican on a bicycle.

Suggested fix: Move the passer-by about 40 to 60 units lower or nearer the bottom edge, or narrow his raised elbows, so the pelican's back wheel or front wheel is always clear. Or shift him to x about 600 so the overlap is shorter.

### [should-fix] Wave drawn as graphic concentric rings and then rain streaks, not the landscape bending

Evidence: 80.1 to 80.5: neat concentric rings of pale yellow, blue and brown dashes centred on his head, which reads as a cartoon sound-wave or radar icon laid over the painting. 80.8/81.1: the tail leaves short straight yellow and blue dashes across the sky and a block of vertical blue dashes on the right hill (81.1, x 1700 to 1900) that read as rain. The sky bands themselves barely bend.

Suggested fix: Let the front displace the existing band strokes (amplitude and colour) instead of adding a separate ring layer. If a ring stays, draw it with the same long band strokes curving along it, and orient trailing strokes along the bands, never radially or vertically.

### [should-fix] Before pose deviates from the brief and reads oddly

Evidence: 79.9/79.95: hands hang as tiny pale stubs at the very hem (screen y about 1040), like feet. The brief asks for 'one hand on the rail'. The head is a dark round blob with a pale crescent sliver of face on the right, which reads as a helmet or mask more than a three-quarter back view.

Suggested fix: Put one hand on the top rail as briefed (arm bent up to rail height), and drop the other hand to mid-thigh. Show more of the cheek and ear on the turned side so the three-quarter turn reads.

### [should-fix] Far figures are paper-doll templates

Evidence: 77.0/79.05: two flat navy rectangles in hats with blank tan circle faces and stick legs, uniform fill and outline. They do not match the chalky stroke treatment of the rest of the picture.

Suggested fix: Build them from vertical crayon strokes like the passer-by's coat. Give them a slight lean and an S-curve, no face disc (a dark head with a small pale cheek), and taper the legs into the planks.

### [should-fix] Sky and fjord are a uniform dash mosaic, not long wavering bands

Evidence: Every frame (e.g. 79.05): strokes are short, similar-length dashes laid in a noisy camouflage pattern. The sky has no five or six clear broad bands and no long continuous curves. The water lacks the looping shoreline, and the hills read like the Starry Night section.

Suggested fix: Lengthen strokes 3 to 5x along the band direction and reduce per-stroke colour noise inside a band so each band reads as one sweep. Add one strong S-curving shoreline in pale ochre. Vary stroke width between sky (broad) and water (thinner, swirling).

### [minor] Railing is a ruled horizontal fence; bicycle lines merge with it

Evidence: 79.9/81.4: top and lower rails are dead-straight horizontals with evenly spaced posts. The thin dark wheel rims and frame cross the dark-brown lower rails and lose contrast (79.9, wheels at x 800 to 1130).

Suggested fix: Give the rails slight waver and a perspective rake. Paint the rails lighter (orange/blue) behind the wheel band, or thicken the bicycle line and add a pale halo stroke.

### [minor] Pelican tail is an odd scribble knot; entry frame rate dips

Evidence: 79.9: the tail at the saddle is a small blue-black looped scribble that reads as a knot. Perf entry section is 57.4 fps with a 60 ms worst gap.

Suggested fix: Draw the tail as two or three short tapering white and grey strokes with the contour. Check what stalls the first entry frames (likely first-use bakes) and pre-bake in init.
